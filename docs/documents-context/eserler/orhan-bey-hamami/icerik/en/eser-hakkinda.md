# Orhan Bey Bath · About (English)

Translation of `../eser-hakkinda.md`, paragraph by paragraph. Sources and trust labels come from the Turkish record.

## Tarihçe | History

Orhan Bey Bath is at Uzunçarşı Caddesi No. 5, Nalbantoğlu neighbourhood, Osmangazi district.

Standing in the historic bazaar area, north of Ulucami and between Emir Han and Koza Han, the bath was built by Orhan Bey in 1340 as part of his complex. According to the endowment deed of Cemaziyelahir 761 / April 1360, drawn up in the name of Sultan Orhan Gazi bin Sultan Osman and certified by the Bursa judge Mevlânâ Burhaneddin İbrahim bin Ali bin Yusuf el-Hemedânî, the Bıçakçılar Bath (Orhan Bath) was dedicated to the Orhan Gazi soup kitchen.

Designed as a double bath, the building was first called Orhan Bey Bath and later by names including Bıçakçılar, Hallaçlar, Hatuniye Bath, Hamam Kahve and Aynalı Çarşı. On the wide garden area deliberately left between it and the mosque, the other part of the complex, Koza Han was built in 1505.

The bath faced various problems after it opened. It was repaired in 1519, abandoned in 1573, and burned in a fire of 1584 together with Emir Han and 35 shops of the Orhan Foundation, after which it was thoroughly repaired. According to a record of Şevval 1037 / June 1628 it came into use as a coffee house, and it was repaired again in AH 1089 / AD 1678.

## Çifte hamamın ortaya çıkışı | How the double bath came to light

Little was known until 1934 about the building, long known and referred to as a bazaar. Research by the architect Sedat Çetintaş, sent to Bursa by the Ministry of Education to study historic buildings, established that it was originally a bath.

However, since only the men's section was standing until then, its being a double bath came to light only through later archival research. In 1943, with funds from the Ministry of Education and financial support from other institutions, work led by the architect Sedat Çetintaş also rebuilt the women's section.

This work established that the water tank and furnace were at the bath's southwest corner, revealing the building's unity as a double bath.

Although it is the first Ottoman bath, it is of medium size, and the men's section in particular has a very good plan. That the women's section was abandoned long ago, and that the furnace and water tank were placed awkwardly far from it, suggests it may have had heating problems.

Although the men's section, used as the Aynalı Bazaar, suffered some alterations, the building's essential form did not change. The alterations include enlarging the inner and outer doors, which damaged the original fabric. The last open cell wall on the axis was also demolished in the 19th century, and a large door opened in its place gave access by stone stairs to the Ulucami side.

In the hot room some original doors were blocked and moved. Tile remains found in excavation and research show that all the domes and vaults were covered with tiles cast in special moulds when the bath was built, and that the building was later covered with a timber roof.

It was established that a door once opened from the last cell in the women's section to the men's side and was later walled up. This is said to be a rare arrangement.

Until 1943 the men's section belonged to the heirs of Gemlikli Nuri Paşa; when one heir put the building up for sale by court order, the General Directorate of Foundations bought it. The women's section has always remained the property of the General Directorate of Foundations.

## Aynalı Çarşı ve plan | The Aynalı Bazaar and the plan

Before the 1958 Bursa Bazaar fire the men's section was known as the "Aynalı Çarşı". In time it lost its function as a bath and was used as a coffee house, and its interior was turned into shops. After a faithful repair following the fire, it was made entirely into a bazaar together with the women's section. With this conversion, new shops were added on the north and west façades, the water tank became a shop, and the furnace was completely demolished to create the bazaar's west entrance.

One of the first examples of Ottoman bath architecture, the building's men's section consists of a square changing room and warm room, and a hot room with four private cells and four iwans. The women's section consists of a square changing room, warm room and two private cells. The walls are of cut stone, and the dome drums of three courses of brick and one of cut stone.

The building now known as the "Old Aynalı Bazaar" has three entrances: north, south and west. The northern entrance is original; the others were opened later.

## Erkekler bölümü | Men's section

The main entrance on the north leads into the square cool room of the men's section. The round-arched entrance is built of three courses of red and white brick and one of küfeki stone. A large double-leaf door of wood and steel opens into the cool room of 11.95 × 11.86 m. Above the door is a round window built of upright bricks set side by side. The space is covered by a dome on an octagonal drum, with squinches as the transition.

Today the interior is divided with wood and steel and used as rows of shops, mainly selling antiques and tourist souvenirs.

In the original plan, the warm room was reached through a door in the south wall of the square cool section, 6.60 × 6.45 m. To the left of this space were a domed private cell and a vaulted toilet, each with its own entrance.

Today these spaces are reached through a wide arch opened in the middle of the warm room; the walls between them have been demolished to line up the shops, and an exit has been added to the west.

The warm room is covered by a dome on a round drum, with Turkish triangles as the transition.

In the original plan this section had four private cells at the corners, three iwans with benches and a heated central stone; a door was built giving access toward Ulucami by a stair of 14 steps starting from the southern iwan.

The other spaces in the hot room are now used as shops.

The main space and the square corner cells of about 3.40 × 3.60 m are domed, and the iwans vaulted.

The whole space takes light from windows in the dome drums and skylights on the domes.

The vaulted water tank west of the hot room is reached through a door in the west wall of the warm room. This door is also used as another exit to the bazaar.

## Kadınlar bölümü | Women's section

The women's section, adjoining the men's on the east, has a square cool room of 10.35 × 10.35 m. It is covered by a dome on an octagonal drum, with Turkish triangles as the transition. The interior is lit by two windows on the north, one on the south, three in the dome drum and a skylight.

A door at the west corner of the cool room's south wall opens into the hot room of 3.50 × 4.20 m, from which two private cells with basins are reached in turn. The bands of the domed cells have rich decoration.

## Bugün | Today

After the restoration an entrance reached by stairs was created on the south, and the women's section came into use by a tradesman. All the bath's domes and vaults are covered with lead and were repaired in 2022–2023.

The Hans District, which includes the bath, was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage site in 2014, together with the Sultans' Complexes and the village of Cumalıkızık.

## Vakıf ve akar | Waqf and akar

The bath is an akar, an income-producing property: under the endowment deed of April 1360, the Bıçakçılar Bath (Orhan Bath) was dedicated to the Orhan Gazi soup kitchen. The women's section has always remained the property of the Foundations; the General Directorate of Foundations bought the men's section in 1943.
