# Tomb of Orhan Gazi · About (English)

Translation of `../eser-hakkinda.md`, paragraph by paragraph. Sources and trust labels come from the Turkish record.

## Tarihçe | History

The tomb is at Yiğitler Caddesi No. 2, Osmangazi neighbourhood, Osmangazi district, within the historic area known as Hisariçi and now called Osmangazi Square. It belongs to Orhan Gazi, the second sultan of the Ottoman state and conqueror of Bursa.

Orhan Bey is known to have carried out the will of his father, Osman Gazi, burying him in one of the old monastery buildings within the walls, later called the "Gümüşlü Kümbet". Orhan Gazi too appears to have been buried after his death in the same complex, probably in the part converted into a mosque. Some construction and repair records in the Bursa judges' registers show that tomb and mosque adjoined each other. As with the Tomb of Osman Gazi, separate foundations were assigned to this tomb.

That the tomb was completely destroyed in the great Bursa earthquake of 1855 is clear from these words in a document:

"…derûn-ı kal'ada kâin an-asl manastır olup şimdi türbe-i şerifeleri bulunan mahal ve civarında medreseleri külliyen münhedim"

In meaning: "…the place in the citadel, originally a monastery and now used as the noble tomb, and the madrasas around it have been completely destroyed."

This document shows that both the building and the madrasa units around it were seriously damaged in the 1855 earthquake.

The present building's inscription has not survived. There is no doubt, however, that it was rebuilt by Sultan Abdülaziz together with the Tomb of Osman Gazi in AH 1280 / AD 1863.

## Mimari | Architecture

The square, single-domed tomb measures 16.80 × 16.80 m, with walls 1.30 metres thick. Entered through a door with a marble frame and a lobed arch, it is centred on a small dome on four columns, as in the Bursa tombs of Murad I Hüdavendigâr and Murad II. The dome is surrounded by a passage with barrel vaults. The Baroque volute decoration seen at the Tomb of Osman Gazi is also used here in rows on the column capitals.

The windows are round-arched, with marble frames and wrought iron grilles. The architectural decoration brings together late Ottoman ornament and classical forms.

The building takes daylight through three windows on each façade and two on the entrance side. Curtains of Hereke fabric give the very plain interior a slight palace air. The inside of the pendentive dome, on pointed arches, is decorated with painted work in cartouches, and the pendentives and window pediments with large Baroque motifs.

## Sandukalar | Cenotaphs

Orhan Gazi's cenotaph, covered with a green broadcloth pall, is surrounded by a brass grille. Above it hangs a blue crystal chandelier with white lamps. The Bursa Olgunlaşma Institute was given the task of preparing a new cover to replace the worn pall; work begun in 2018 was finished in 2023. The new cover was laid over the original pall without removing it.

The lack of a keeper's room, the plain window grilles, the undecorated cover, the small chandelier and the absence of an inscription show that the building was arranged far more simply than the Tomb of Osman Gazi.

Like the Tomb of Osman Gazi, it is known to have had a porch with two small columns and a rectangular entrance hall. These were rebuilt in keeping with the original building during recent restoration.

The tomb holds twenty more cenotaphs besides Orhan Gazi's. Apart from his, which stands in the middle on a marble base within the brass grille, they are placed on brick platforms that look like marble.

The cenotaphs of Orhan Gazi, his wife Nilüfer Hatun, his son Şehzade Kasım, Yıldırım Bayezid's daughter Fatma Sultan, Cem Sultan's son Abdullah and II. Bayezid's son Şehzade Korkut have been identified. It is not known to whom the others belong.

## Eski yapı ve onarımlar | The old building and repairs

Documents in the judges' registers also show that the earlier building was covered by several domes, whereas the new building was given a single dome. Of the old building, only floor mosaic pieces such as entrelac motifs and panel borders can be seen.

Repaired many times for various reasons, the building had partial or extensive repairs in 1615, 1791, 1808, 1813 and 1816–1818 before being rebuilt, finally by Sultan Abdülaziz in 1868.

The area containing the tomb was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage site in 2014, together with the Hans District, the Sultans' Complexes and the village of Cumalıkızık.

## Vakıf ve akar | Waqf and akar

As with the Tomb of Osman Gazi, separate foundations were assigned to this tomb.
