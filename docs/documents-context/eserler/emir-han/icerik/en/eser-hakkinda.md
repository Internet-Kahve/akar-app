# Emir Han · About (English)

Translation of `../eser-hakkinda.md`, paragraph by paragraph. Sources and trust labels come from the Turkish record.

## Tarihçe | History

Emir Han is at Uzunçarşı Caddesi No. 11, Nalbantoğlu neighbourhood, Osmangazi district, at the northeast corner of Ulucami.

It was built by Gazi Orhan Bey as part of his complex in AH 740 / AD 1339–40, in the area where the Horse Market was held. The building is considered the first commercial element of the nucleus created to strengthen Bursa's claim to be a new centre of trade after its conquest in 1326. This process developed over the following years and achieved its aim. Emir Han reinterpreted the Seljuk caravanserai for the city's needs and is considered among the first examples of its type in Ottoman city han architecture.

Historical records call the han by various names, including "Bey Hanı", "Emir Hanı", "Hân-ı Bezzâziye", "Eski Bedesten", "Kepenekçiler Çarşısı", "Sultan Hanı" and "Kervansaray". It was also known as "Bahar Kapanı" because the bazaar's scales in Bursa were here.

Emir Han is considered Bursa's first bedesten. After the new bedesten was built under Yıldırım Bayezid, it was called the "Old Bedesten".

Food consumed in the city and all goods bought and sold were weighed in this han, and the resulting weighing fees formed its source of income.

The benches built to hold the scales used from the conquest of Bursa until 1781 can be seen at the han's entrance.

## Mimari | Architecture

The han, about 45 × 45 m, has a central courtyard of around 650 m² surrounded by arcades on wide piers of cut stone and brick. Its walls alternate two courses of brick with one of küfeki stone.

The two-storey building has 36 separate rooms on the lower floor and 38 on the upper. The façades use rubble stone, while the arcades are brick and finished with two rows of saw-tooth eaves.

Apart from the domes at the four corners of the first-floor arcades, all spaces are vaulted. Behind the lower arcades, now closed with glass and wood partitions, rectangular barrel-vaulted rooms line up. At the corners are other rooms reached by diagonal passages.

Different arch shapes are used in the building. The lower arches of the south wing's arcade are semicircular, while pointed arches are used on the other three sides and on the upper floor.

The piers of the south arcade on the ground floor, unlike the rest, are of cut stone, with round arches in alternating masonry.

The lower rooms are windowless and were used as protected stores for goods brought by caravans. The upper rooms, with hearths, lodged the merchants. Thirty-four of these rooms have one window and two have two, while the two rooms at the southeast corner have none.

The han has two entrances, one from the Ulucami courtyard and the other from the Covered Bazaar; two stone stairs on the north side, on the Covered Bazaar side, link its floors. South of the han there were rectangular vaulted stables, later removed; Ulucami was built on this area in 1399.

## Onarımlar | Repairs

It suffered fires and earthquakes in 1544, 1634, 1674, 1788, 1855 and 1958 and each time underwent long and costly repairs, yet has come down to us with its essence and original function intact.

One of the most extensive alterations was carried out in 1962–63, after the Great Bazaar Fire of 1958. Its roof is covered with lead today; it is known to have been tiled for a time.

## Bugün | Today

Today the upper floor mainly houses booksellers, sellers of pilgrimage and religious goods, jewellers and similar tradesmen. The lower floor has mostly places to eat and drink and shops of various kinds.

Always lively with trade, the han, with its jet fountain and a courtyard shaded by historic plane trees, remains a pleasant meeting place for conversation over tea, coffee and sour grape juice.

## Vakıf ve akar | Waqf and akar

Food consumed in the city and goods bought and sold were weighed in this han, and the weighing fees formed its source of income; the scales were used until 1781.

Orhan Gazi's endowment deed of 1360 lists an "Old Bedesten" among the properties given to the soup kitchen; one of Emir Han's historical names is "Old Bedesten". That the building in the deed is Emir Han is inferred from these two facts.
