# Koza Han · About (English)

Translation of `../eser-hakkinda.md`, paragraph by paragraph. Sources and trust labels come from the Turkish record.

## Tarihçe | History

Koza Han, in the Osmangazi district of Bursa, stands in the Bazaar and Hans District between Orhan Mosque and Ulucami. Through history it has been known as "Yeni Han", "Beylik Han", "Hân-ı Cedîd-i Âmire", "Hân-ı Cedîd-i Evvel", "Simkeş Han", "Sırmakeş Han", "Beylik Kârbansaray" and "Eski Yeni Han"; after Pirinç Han was built it came to be known as Eski Yeni Han.

Koza Han was built by Sultan II. Bayezid to provide an income for his complex in Istanbul. Begun in March 1489, it was completed and opened on 30 September 1491. Its architect was Abdülûlâ bin Pulad Şah, its building supervisor Şücâ bin Karaca, and its clerks Hacı Yusuf bin Abdullah, Hayreddin bin Hamza and Kemal Bey.

Still commercially lively today, Koza Han stands on the same axis as Orhan Mosque, Emir Han and Ulucami, buildings that opened up the city, which shows that the city's trade-centred pattern of settlement continued.

## Mimari | Architecture

In keeping with the classic Ottoman han plan, the han consists of rooms on two levels around a courtyard and a stable and storage section against the east façade. The single-storey stable adjoins the han on the east. A passage 3.66 m wide in Koza Han's east wall leads to the second courtyard, where the stables were.

The stable courtyard is a rectangular area of about 22 × 30 m surrounded by arches, though the arches on its east side were later walled up. It is surrounded by arches on eighteen piers of various sizes. The whole stable space is carried by forty-five piers. Three separate doors lead from the stables directly to Koza Han, Orhan Mosque and the bazaar.

In the middle of the courtyard is a free-standing mosque of the kind called a kiosk mosque, seen earlier in some Seljuk caravanserais. An archival document shows that a second, wooden mosque was built next to it because it was too small for the congregation, but that building has not survived.

## Onarımlar ve bugün | Repairs, and today

A document of AH 1040 / AD 1630 records repairs to the han's water channels and notes six toilets on the upper floor. These have not survived.

The han's wooden upper arcade was repaired in the 1950s by the Society of Friends of Bursa Antiquities, which also overhauled the stables. Used today as a coffee house and kiosk, this section is one of the rare stable buildings to survive from the Ottoman period.

Koza Han has become one of the busiest trading areas in the Bursa Bazaar. Silk businesses occupy the upper floor, and tradesmen selling various goods the lower.

The han's courtyard is used as a tea garden and is very popular.

## Köşk mescit | Kiosk mosque

The mosque is at Uzunçarşı Caddesi No. 59, Şehreküstü neighbourhood, Osmangazi district.

Koza Han was built in October 1491 by Sultan II. Bayezid as an endowment for his works in Istanbul. It has lost none of its importance and remains a busy centre of trade.

The Koza Han Kiosk Mosque is among the rare kiosk mosques built in the middle of han courtyards in Bursa to survive in original form. Another is the Fidan Han Kiosk Mosque. The mosques of Pirinç Han and Balibey Han no longer exist.

The octagonal building has a door on one side and a plaster mihrab with a muqarnas hood on another. Built of cut stone, the mosque rests on round arches rising on eight hexagonal piers. It has a lower window on each side, and the arch panels have grilles. Its dome is covered with lead.

The pool with taps along the building's lower edges is a sixteen-sided fountain. In the middle of the pool is an eight-sided pier with a stalactite capital and a pipe on each face, through which water pours into the pool. Ablution taps are in the part of the fountain beneath the stairwell and the mihrab.

The mosque is reached by a marble stair of fourteen steps, with marble balustrades and geometric grilles. It has a wooden door and a wooden floor. The other six corners have plaster windows, closed outside with iron bars, each with a pointed-arched pediment decorated with a honeycomb of pentagons.

At one time, as the congregation grew, Hoca Mir had an additional wooden mosque built beside it, but that building has not survived.

## Mescidin onarımları | The mosque's repairs

Repaired in 1584, 1627, 1643, 1671 and 1778, the Koza Han Kiosk Mosque had its most extensive repair in 1948, by the Society of Friends of Bursa Antiquities with the help of the merchants. Records in the Bursa court registers showed that the mosque, by then covered with a wooden roof, had originally been domed. It was therefore restored with its dome, using bricks from the debris of Tahtakale (Bayezid Paşa) Han. This was also the society's first serious work. Since only two of the outer windows had survived in original form, the others were made after them. The windows were also repaired during the work, with frames of walnut. The master of the stained-glass windows inside was İsmail Sönmez, who worked on many buildings in Bursa.

The kiosk mosque in the courtyard of Koza Han was restored in 2008 by a private organisation. Inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2014, this elegant building is an important part of Bursa's historic Bazaar and Hans District.

The work carries elegant traces of Anatolian Seljuk architecture and is also one of the finest examples of the early Ottoman period.

## Vakıf ve akar | Waqf and akar

Koza Han is an akar, an income-producing property: II. Bayezid had it built to provide an income for his complex in Istanbul; the Site Management sheet calls these buildings "his mosque and madrasa in Istanbul". Like the Pirinç, Fidan and Bali Bey hans, it is one of the large hans planned in the bazaar to provide income for charitable institutions. It remains one of the busiest trading areas in the Bursa Bazaar, with silk businesses upstairs, various tradesmen below and a tea garden in the courtyard.
