# Orhan Bey Mosque · 3D part cards (English)

Translation of `../parcalar.md`.

| Parçalar | Title (EN) | Text (EN) |
|---|---|---|
| Cami_Govde, Cami_Ortu | The mosque and its façades | The mosque was built in 1339–40 as part of Orhan Bey's complex; only the mosque, Emir Han and Orhan Bath have survived. Its walls are of rubble stone with bricks set horizontally and vertically between. The east façade has lozenge decoration in brick and stone, and the west façade chequered patterns. |
| Kubbe_Ana, Kubbe_Pencereler | Central dome | The central space is covered by a dome on an octagonal drum with Turkish triangles. The seven round-arched windows with plaster grilles in and around the dome have decoration with rumi and palmette motifs. The mosque's domes and roof are entirely covered with lead. |
| Orta_Mekan, Pandantifler, Ic_Kubbeler, Ic_Zemin | Central space | A double-leaf wooden door leads into the central space of 8.90 × 9.90 metres, with two raised platforms at the sides and the muezzins' gallery above them. The interior takes light from paired windows, one above the other, on the east, west and mihrab walls, and from the windows in the dome. The lower windows, originally rectangular, were given pointed arches in the 1905 alterations. |
| Mihrap_Eyvani, Kubbe_Mihrap | Main prayer space | Four marble steps lead up from the central space to the mihrab section of 9.64 × 8.83 metres. This is the part art history calls the "main prayer space" only in mosques with hospices or zawiyas. Its dome, reached through prismatic squinches and Turkish triangles, has a lozenge motif in a red medallion and palmette decoration. |
| Cami_Mihrap, Ic_Mihrap | Plaster mihrab | The mihrab, a model for later sultans' mosques in Bursa, is considered one of the finest examples of advanced plasterwork. Its gilded hood of seven tiers of muqarnas is decorated with rumi and palmette motifs and lotus flowers. Having survived largely unchanged compared with other mosques makes it an important architectural asset. |
| Tabhaneler, Kubbe_Tabhaneler | Hospice rooms | East and west of the central space are domed hospice rooms of about 6.05 × 9.10 metres, separated by pointed arches and reached by one step each. With this arrangement the mosque is a characteristic example of early Ottoman architecture, combining worship and the functions of a dervish lodge in one building. In 1905 the partition walls of the rooms were removed and they were joined to the mosque; the eastern one is now the women's gallery. |
| Cami_SonCemaat | Late-comers' portico | The five-bay portico measures 25.80 × 6.30 metres and is entirely domed. It is among the first monumental late-comers' porticoes in Anatolian Turkish and Ottoman architecture. Its sides are not blind: each opens outward through two pointed arches on reused composite capitals. |
| Giris_Koridoru | Entrance and Orhan Library | Either side of the small domed entrance landing is a room. The western room became the Orhan Library in 1863 with books brought by Ahmed Vefik Paşa; lasting until 1969, this library is the basis of today's İnebey Manuscript Library. The marble pediment above the main door bears the repair inscription from the reign of Çelebi Mehmed. |
| Minber | Minbar and preacher's chair | The minbar and preacher's chair are wood and plainly crafted. Historic candlesticks stand to the right and left of the mihrab. |
| Minare | Minaret | The polygonal minaret at the mosque's northeast corner stands out for its elegant brickwork and occasional star motifs. Its single balcony is decorated beneath with two rows of stalactites. Its cap was built of cut stone in 1899 and covered with lead again in the 2020–2023 restoration. |
| Sadirvan | Fountain | The fountain in the mosque's courtyard was returned to its original form, with its wooden roof and decorated dome, in the 2020–2023 restoration. |
