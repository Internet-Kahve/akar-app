# Orhan Bey Mosque · About (English)

Translation of `../eser-hakkinda.md`, paragraph by paragraph. Sources and trust labels come from the Turkish record.

## Tarihçe | History

Orhan Bey Mosque is at Belediye Caddesi No. 7, Nalbantoğlu neighbourhood, Osmangazi district.

In the historic Bazaar and Hans District, the mosque stands east of Ulucami and west of the historic town hall. It was built in AH 740 / AD 1339–40 as part of the complex commissioned by Orhan Bey, son of Osman Bey, founder of the Ottoman state.

Of this group of buildings, one of the first Ottoman complexes, only Orhan Mosque, Emir Han and Orhan Bath have survived. The soup kitchen and school have disappeared over time.

Gazi Orhan Bey came to the head of the state in 1326 when his elder brother Alaaddin Bey (Paşa) gave up his claim. He declared Bursa, conquered that same year, the capital and moved his seat of government here; with this complex built outside the walls he developed and enlarged the city and made it a centre of attraction.

He increased the territory he inherited from his father from 6,000 km² sixteenfold, to 95,000 km². Orhan Gazi died in 1360 at the age of 79 and was buried in his tomb within the citadel, in the Monastery of Saint Elias, which he had converted into a mosque and madrasa after the conquest.

According to the 28-page endowment deed of Cemaziyelahir 761 / April 1360, drawn up in the name of Sultan Orhan Gazi bin Sultan Osman and certified by the Bursa judge Mevlânâ Burhaneddin İbrahim bin Ali bin Yusuf el-Hemedânî, some villages, the Old Bedesten, the shops, rooms and houses beside and around it, and the Bıçakçılar Bath were dedicated to the Orhan Gazi soup kitchen.

During the 31-day siege of Bursa by Karamanoğlu Mehmed Bey in 1413, this complex was burned and damaged along with many other buildings outside the walls; it was then repaired by Sultan Çelebi Mehmed and reopened for worship.

The five-line framed repair inscription above the main door reads:

"Emera bi-hâzihi'l 'imârati'ş-şerîfeti Sultânu'l-guzâti ve'l-mücahidîn Orhan Beg ibni Osman Beg -tâbe serâhumâ- fî seneti erbaîne ve seb'a mieti (AH 740 / AD 1339–40) ve haraka veledü Karamân sümme emera'n-nâziru ve hüve'l vezîru'l-kebîru Bayezîd Paşa- bi-işâreti's Sultan ibni's-Sultan Muhammed ibni Bayezîd Hân -hullide sultânuhu- fî seneti işrîn ve semâne-mie" (AH 820)

In meaning: "The building of this noble structure was ordered by Orhan Bey, sultan of the warriors and fighters for the faith, son of Osman Bey, may God exalt the souls of both, in the year AD 1339–40. Then the son of Karaman launched an attack. Later the overseer of this building and grand vizier Bayezid Paşa, at the command of Sultan Mehmed son of Bayezid Han, the sultan son of a sultan, may God make his reign lasting, ordered it rebuilt in AD 1417."

## Plan ve cepheler | Plan and façades

Among the Ottomans' early mosques with hospices or zawiyas, this building stands out for its symmetrical plan. The side spaces are covered with mirror vaults and the other parts with domes. The five-bay late-comers' portico measures 25.80 × 6.30 m and is entirely domed. The main space consists of two successive square sections, with rectangular hospice rooms to their east and west. Two rectangular vaulted rooms adjoin the hospice rooms on the north. With this arrangement the mosque is one of the characteristic examples of early Ottoman architecture, combining worship and the functions of a dervish lodge in one building.

The brick-paved platforms on either side of the portico are about 90 cm above ground and surrounded by iron railings. In this arcade, among the first monumental late-comers' porticoes in Anatolian Turkish and Ottoman architecture, the sides are not blind: each opens outward through two pointed arches on reused composite capitals. These spatial features are among the architectural elements showing that the Seljuk tradition continued.

The walls are of rubble stone with bricks set horizontally and vertically between. Arches of brick and stone courses, decoration enlivening the façade with different brick patterns, and courses placed at the corners enrich the mosque's exterior.

All wall surfaces are enlivened by two pointed-arched mouldings running from the ground to the saw-tooth eaves, with one window or two, one above the other, opened within them. The domes and roof are entirely covered with lead.

The transition to the twin side arches and the upper arch is made with mouldings showing Byzantine influence. A decorative order is created with lozenge patterns of brick and stone on the east façade and chequered motifs on the west. Some hold that this decoration may date from the reign of Çelebi Mehmed.

In the south wall of the arcaded area, beside the entrance door, are two rectangular windows on each side set within pointed arches. These windows are known to date from the 19th century. Rumi and palmette motifs appear above the window arches and are repeated inside them.

Either side of the small domed entrance landing are two rooms entered through pointed-arched doors. The western room became the Orhan Library in 1863, when Ahmed Vefik Paşa had the books in the Ahmed-i Gazzî Lodge brought here. Lasting until 1969, the library grew as many manuscripts and printed works came from other places, and it is important as the basis of today's İnebey Manuscript Library. The room east of the hall was once a public library store. Today these spaces serve as an imam's room, a muezzin's room, the landing of the minaret stair and a passage to the stairs leading to the lead roof.

## Harim ve tabhaneler | Prayer hall and hospice rooms

The round-arched marble pediment above the depressed-arched, marble-framed door bears the mosque's repair inscription from the reign of Çelebi Mehmed. A double-leaf wooden door leads into the central space of the prayer hall, 8.90 × 9.90 m, covered by a dome on an octagonal drum with Turkish triangles.

The seven round-arched windows with plaster grilles in and around the dome have decoration of rumi and palmette motifs. To the right and left of the space are two raised platforms, with the muezzins' gallery above them.

East and west of the central space are hospice sections, separated by pointed arches and reached by one step each. These rectangular spaces of about 6.05 × 9.10 m are domed. The insides of the domes and arches are decorated with rumi and palmette motifs.

Each is lit by two pointed-arched windows; the eastern one is now used as the women's gallery. On 1 September 1905, under Governor Reşid Mümtaz Paşa and by order of Abdülhamid II, the partition walls of the hospice rooms were removed and they were joined to the mosque, and the window in the middle of the east wall was turned into a door.

During the extensive restoration of 2020–2023, this door was made a direct outside entrance to the women's gallery. The alteration inscription of AH 1323 / AD 1905 recording the door's opening, removed to a museum some time ago, was also put back.

The text of the inscription, an example of Ottoman calligraphy, reads: "Veliyy-i ni'met-i âlî-menkabet zînet-bahş-ı makâm-ı hilâfet ve saltanat es-Sultân ibni's-Sultân Es-Sultân Gâzî Abdülhamîd Hân hazretleri me'ser-i bî-gâyât-ı hilâfet-penâhîlerine Bir zamîme-i mübâreke olarak ecdâd-ı hümâyûn-ı cenâb-ı mülûkânelerinden Fâtih-i Burusa Cennet-mekân Gâzî Orhan hazretlerinin işbu câmi-i şerîflerini dahî tecdîden inşâ buyurmuşlardır. 1 Receb sene 1323."

In meaning: "His Majesty Sultan Gazi Abdülhamid Han, sultan of sultans, who adorns the seat of the caliphate and sultanate with his high merits and protects the Islamic world under his blessed care, as a gift to the splendour of the caliphate, ordered the rebuilding of this noble mosque of Gazi Orhan Bey, conqueror of Bursa and dweller in paradise, an ancestor of his exalted dynasty. This good work was carried out on 1 Receb 1323."

From the central part of the prayer hall a marble stair of four steps leads up to the mihrab section of 9.64 × 8.83 m, which art historians call the "main prayer space" only in mosques with hospices or zawiyas.

These two sections are separated by a wide pointed arch on broad side imposts. The dome on an octagonal drum, reached through prismatic squinches and Turkish triangles, has a lozenge motif in a red medallion and palmette decoration. The prismatic squinches also show rumi and palmette decoration.

The interior is lit by paired lower and upper windows on the east, west and mihrab walls and by three windows in the dome. The upper windows are round-arched; the lower ones, originally fully rectangular, took their present pointed-arched form in the alterations of 1905.

## Mihrap | Mihrab

The magnificent mihrab, a model for later sultans' mosques in Bursa, is considered one of the finest examples of advanced plasterwork.

Its gilded hood of seven tiers of muqarnas is surrounded by rumi and palmette motifs and lotus flowers. Within the medallion decoration of the semicircular mihrab niche, verse 84 of the Surah Isrâ appears in mirrored thuluth script:

"Kul küllün ya'melu alâ şâkiletihî fe rabbuküm a'lemu bimen hüve ehdâ sebîlen –

Say: "Everyone acts according to his own nature. Your Lord knows best who is most rightly guided."

That the mihrab has survived with less change than those of other mosques makes it an important architectural asset. Historic candlesticks still stand to its right and left.

## Minber ve minare | Minbar and minaret

The minbar and preacher's chair are wood and plainly crafted.

The polygonal minaret at the mosque's northeast corner stands out for its elegant brickwork, with star motifs in places. The underside of its single balcony is decorated with two rows of stalactites. Its cap, said to have been lead-covered earlier, was built of cut stone in 1899 and covered with lead again in the restoration of 2020–2023.

## Onarımlar ve bugün | Repairs, and today

The mosque was partly or wholly repaired for various reasons in 1576, 1619, 1628–29, 1732 and 1773. It was damaged by fire in 1782 and, after the great Bursa fire of 1801, thoroughly restored in 1812 under the governor of Bursa, Ahmed Aziz Paşa. After the 1855 earthquake it was restored again in the 1860s, coordinated by the French architect Léon Parvillée, brought by Ahmet Vefik Paşa.

The fountain in the mosque's courtyard was returned to its original form, with its wooden roof and decorated dome, in the restoration of 2020–2023.

The Hans District, which includes the mosque, was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage site in 2014, together with the Sultans' Complexes and the village of Cumalıkızık.

## Vakıf ve akar | Waqf and akar

According to the endowment deed of April 1360, some villages, the Old Bedesten and the shops, rooms and houses around it, and the Bıçakçılar (Orhan) Bath were dedicated to the Orhan Gazi soup kitchen. The mosque, Emir Han and Orhan Bath survive from the complex; the soup kitchen and school have disappeared.
