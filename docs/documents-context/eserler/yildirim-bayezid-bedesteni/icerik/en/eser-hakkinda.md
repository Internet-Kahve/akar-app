## Bursa çarşısının doğuşu | The birth of the Bursa bazaar

When the Ottomans conquered Bursa on 6 April 1326, the settlement was limited to the area within the citadel. To the east a neighbourhood ran from Çakır Bath to Zafer Square, and to the west lay the Gece neighbourhood at the foot of the walls. At that date the citadel held about two thousand houses, seven neighbourhoods, seven mihrabs (small mosques), a bath, a market and twenty shops, while the area where Emir Han now stands was used as a horse market.

In the Turkish-Islamic urban tradition, a settlement needed a mosque for Friday prayers and a marketplace to gain the status of a city. On these criteria, mosques, bedestens and soup kitchens became central elements of Ottoman city planning. The city's transport network took shape around these core buildings, and the functional unity between them was preserved.

Rising to capital after the conquest of 1326, Bursa soon became one of the most important trading centres in Anatolia. In this development, Emir Han, built by Orhan Gazi, formed the first core of the market and played an important commercial role before the bedesten was built.

The buildings of the complex had a scattered layout in this period. Emir Han was set apart from buildings for worship and education and placed near the bath, while Koza Han was built under II. Bayezid in the space between the han and the mosque. In time these two hans came to be surrounded by the bedesten, arasta and various hans, and the area became a many-sided trading centre.

## Bedesten | Bedesten

The first bedesten built in the Ottoman Empire, the Yıldırım Bayezid Bedesten was commissioned by Yıldırım Bayezid in the 14th century as the city rapidly became a centre of trade. It set an example not only for Bursa but for all later Ottoman bedestens. Placed near the great Friday mosque, it was meant to provide income for the foundation and also formed the core of the market district. In plan it resembles the bedestens of Istanbul and especially Edirne. Its layout shows that it lies on an axis parallel with Emir Han and Orhan Bath.

The central space measures 15.00 × 56.50 metres inside. The roof consists of 14 domes on six piers. The commercial spaces are 32 storerooms in rows running east to west, with shops arranged in front of them and around the piers. All these units are covered by barrel vaults. The Bedesten is entered through gates on its four façades, each opening into a domed entrance space. Today there are 68 shops around it, but these have no direct connection with its original layout. A fountain with a jet at the centre points to the space's inner courtyard arrangement.

According to Evliya Çelebi, the four sides of the Bedesten were surrounded by the jewellers' market. Immediately around this area were the markets of silk merchants, turban makers, skullcap makers, yarn sellers, cloth sellers, haberdashers, quilt makers, Gelincikçiler, cap makers and cotton fluffers. These trades survive today, if to a limited extent. Again according to Evliya Çelebi, the markets were originally roofed with lead, with iron windows in places. Fountains at every corner reflect the social side of the market's infrastructure.

An extension of the trading areas around the Bedesten, the Saraçhane Market, now considered part of the Covered Bazaar, was also a lively centre where different trades worked together. After a great fire, however, it lost this role and never regained its old vitality.

## Yapı ve onarım | Structure and repair

Stone and brick were used together in the Bedesten's construction. Over time the building wore down, and it was badly damaged and left unusable in the great Covered Bazaar fire of 1958. In 1960 it underwent a thorough restoration, faithful to its original architecture. Today the Bedesten survives as a part of the Bursa Covered Bazaar and is largely home to jewellers' workshops. The sign "Bedesten Jewellers' Market" above one of the gates clearly shows this use.

## Bugün | Today

Today the Bedesten continues to function as part of the Bursa Covered Bazaar, known especially for its jewellers' workshops. The sign "Bedesten Jewellers' Market" above one of the gates documents this use.

## Vakıf ve akar | Waqf and akar

The Bedesten is an akar, an income-producing property: Yıldırım Bayezid had it built to provide Ulucami with an income and placed it near the Friday mosque. Besides providing income for the foundation, it formed the core of the market district; today's Bursa Covered Bazaar grew around this core. Large hans later built in the bazaar, such as Koza Han, were likewise planned to provide income for other charitable institutions.
