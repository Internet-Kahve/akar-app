# Ulucami · 3D part cards (English)

Translation of `../parcalar.md`.

| Parçalar | Title (EN) | Text (EN) |
|---|---|---|
| Tac_Kapi, Portal_Silmeleri, Giris_Cikintili_Blok | Crown portal · Booksellers' Gate | The mosque's main entrance is the marble crown portal on the north façade, known as the Booksellers' Gate. Its hood is framed by a pointed arch and made up of eleven rows of muqarnas. Beside this northern gate there is also an entrance on each of the east and west façades. The north façade was damaged in the 1958 Bursa Bazaar fire; the mosque was last restored in 2009. |
| Kapi_Dogu | Bazaar Gate | The entrance on the east façade is known as the Bazaar Gate. An additional door was once opened at the southeast corner, where the sultan's lodge stood for a time, but it was closed on the grounds that it was a later addition. |
| Kapi_Bati | Mufti's (Court) Gate | The entrance on the west façade is known as the Mufti's or Court Gate. Ulucami is entered from the north, east and west; the qibla wall to the south has no door. |
| Cephe_Kuzey, Cephe_Guney, Cephe_Dogu, Cephe_Bati, Pencere_Girintileri | Façades and windows | Ulucami is a nearly square rectangular building of about 55 × 69 metres. Its façades, enlivened by large mouldings, have fifty-eight windows: twenty-six in the lower row and thirty-two in the upper. The walls are stone. |
| Kubbeler_19, Cati | Twenty domes | Twenty domes, eleven metres across, cover the building; inside, they rest on twelve square piers. The mosque is the largest and best-known Ottoman example of the "ulucami" type, made up of many domes of the same diameter. Evliya Çelebi reports that the domes were covered with lead. The central dome was left open to let in light and air. |
| Kasnak_Pencereleri | Dome windows | Each dome rises on a drum with windows. According to the record of the 1732 repairs, the glass and wire of one hundred and twenty-nine dome windows across the twenty domes were repaired. |
| Alemler | Finials | Each dome is crowned with a finial. Evliya Çelebi likens these finials to a shining sun. |
| Sadirvan_Kubbe, Sadirvan_Iskeleti | Open dome | The top of the twentieth, central dome was left open to ventilate the mosque and bring in daylight; the pool fountain lies beneath it. In Evliya Çelebi's time this opening was covered with a brass wire mesh. In a photograph taken from above it now appears closed by a glazed, ribbed cover. |
| Ic_Sadirvan_Havuz | Pool fountain | One of the striking elements of Ulucami's layout is the pool fountain beneath the open dome. The pool is faced with Marmara marble slabs; each side has a Baroque motif and a tap. Pınarbaşı water flows from the three-tiered fountain in its centre, an arrangement that both pleases the eye and cools the space. |
| Payeler, Kemerler | Piers and arches | The domes, eleven metres across, rest on twelve square piers. The twelve masonry piers are joined to one another by pointed arches. Each face of the piers carries calligraphy. The muezzins' gallery leans against the western of the two piers facing the mihrab; it was built in 1549 by Mustafa Efendi, trustee of the Yıldırım Bayezid foundations. |
| Levhalar | Calligraphy and panels | Ulucami has about one hundred and seventy calligraphic works, written on the walls or hung as panels; the interior is almost a museum of calligraphy. Each face of the piers carries examples of the Beautiful Names of God in monumental thuluth and kufic. The inscriptions are by renowned calligraphers such as Abdülfettah Efendi, Kazasker Mustafa İzzet Efendi, Sâmi Efendi and Mehmed Şefik Bey. On the qibla wall, to the right of the mihrab, is a large letter "vav". The panels in the 3D model only mark their places; the inscriptions themselves are in the photographs. |
| Mihrap | Mihrab | The mihrab stands at the centre of the south (qibla) wall; it measures about 5.13 × 8.11 metres and projects 29 centimetres from the wall. Its present decoration was commissioned by Tevfik Paşa, who was exiled to Bursa in 1862. Surrounded by four friezes, the mihrab has a niche hood with eight rows of muqarnas. Its pediment bears a monumental inscription dated 1275 in gold on green, and its spandrels have decoration in green, purple and gold. |
| Minber | Minbar | The wooden minbar was made in the kündekâri technique, with interlocking pieces and no nails or glue. Its master was Hâc Muhammed bin Abdülazîz ibnü'd-Dakkî. According to the inventory it consists of 6,666 pieces. The inscription on the minbar's door shows that the mosque was completed in 1399–1400. |
| Minare_1 | West minaret | Of Ulucami's two minarets, the western one was built by Yıldırım Bayezid. Its base and plinth are marble and its round shaft is brick; the balcony rests on six rows of stalactites. The conical caps were originally wood covered with lead and were rebuilt in stone after the last fire. The minaret is about 48 metres tall. |
| Minare_2 | East minaret · "Stumpy Minaret" | The eastern minaret was planned from the start but left unfinished for some time, which is why it was called the "Güdük" (Stumpy) Minaret. Like the western one, it has a brick shaft on a marble base and plinth. It is thought to have been completed under Çelebi Sultan Mehmed or Yavuz Sultan Selim. |
| Avlu_Sadirvan_Dogu, Avlu_Sadirvan_Bati | Courtyard fountains | Three water structures stand in a line in the mosque's northern courtyard. The eastern and western ablution fountains, with eight wooden posts, trefoil arches and onion domes, were renewed in 2011 in keeping with old photographs. |
| Avlu_Mermer_Cesme | Courtyard fountain | This is the marble fountain in the middle of the three water structures in the northern courtyard. According to the inventory, the fountain at the mosque's southwest corner and the courtyard fountains were built with donations from Şeyhülislam Karaçelebizâde Abdülaziz Efendi. |
