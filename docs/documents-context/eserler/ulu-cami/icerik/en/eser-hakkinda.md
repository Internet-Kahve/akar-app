# Ulucami · About (English)

Translation of `../eser-hakkinda.md`, paragraph by paragraph. Sources and trust labels come from the Turkish record.

## Tarihçe | History

Ulucami is on Cami Sokak, Atatürk Caddesi, Şehreküstü neighbourhood, Osmangazi district.

It is the first great mosque the Ottomans built in Bursa, and for six centuries it remained the largest in the city. In his Seyahatnâme, Evliya Çelebi described Ulucami as the "Hagia Sophia of Bursa". After three years of construction, the mosque opened on a Friday in 1399.

Hamîdüddin-i Aksarâyî, known as "Somuncu Baba", who earned his living in Bursa baking and selling bread in his own oven, attended the opening and gave a commentary on the Surah Fatiha at seven different levels.

The mosque's first imam was Süleyman Çelebi, author of Vesîletü'n-Necât, popularly known as the Mevlid.

The date on the minbar shows that the mosque and madrasa were completed in AH 802 / AD 1399–1400. The architect of this work commissioned by Sultan Yıldırım Bayezid is not known for certain; it is thought to have been built by Ali Neccâr or Hacı İvaz Paşa. The Bursa yearbooks state that "from its general character it is understood that the architect who made the building was Greek", but the accuracy of this is disputed.

At the planning stage the mosque was designed as a complex of mosque, madrasa, bath, bedesten, shops and staff lodgings. Over time fountains, a timekeeper's room, a school, and rooms for the muezzin and timekeeper were added around it. In recent years, however, most of these additions have been removed, the madrasa has come to be used as shops and stores, and the bath has lost its function.

Early Ottoman architecture produced square, single-domed small mosques as well as multi-functional mosques with zawiyas or hospices, known as the "Bursa type". Mosques with the plan called "ulucami" or "multi-domed" were also built in this period.

These great Friday mosques, called "Masjid-i Jami" in other Islamic countries, were known as "ulucami" in Ottoman culture. Bursa Ulucami is the largest and best-known example in Ottoman architecture of the "ulucami" form, made up of many domes of the same diameter, first applied under Murad I Hüdavendigâr.

The mosque continues the earlier great mosques of Plovdiv and Gelibolu. It was also the forerunner of the later nine-domed Molla Arap Mosque in Bursa and Old Mosque in Edirne, and of the six-domed Zincirlikuyu Mosque in Karagümrük, Istanbul, and Piyalepaşa Mosque.

When Orhan Bey's han and mosque of 1339 could no longer meet needs some sixty years later, Yıldırım Bayezid built Ulucami parallel to Orhan Mosque and the Bedesten opposite Emir Han. Although the Orhan Complex had already set the direction of Bursa's growth, the Bedesten built by Yıldırım Bayezid made the market's focal point clearer.

Despite many detailed studies and publications on Bursa Ulucami, a turning point in Turkish mosque architecture, the identity of its architect has not been firmly established. It is thought to have been built by Hacı İvaz bin Ahî Bayezid, architect of the Green Complex, by Ali Neccâr, or by a non-Muslim architect taken prisoner by Yıldırım Bayezid at the Battle of Nicopolis.

One of those taken prisoner at Nicopolis, the German-born Johann Schiltberger, spent about seven years at the Ottoman court until he was captured by Timur at the Battle of Ankara, and probably witnessed the building of Ulucami. His memoirs, however, do not mention it.

Another person named as the architect of Ulucami is the architect Hacı Alâaddin of Konya.

The carved wooden inscription above the door of the minbar reads:

"Mimmâ umile bi-resmi es-Sultânu'l-muazzam Bâyezîd bin Murâd Hân bi-târîh-i sene isneyni ve semâne mi'e."

In meaning: "This building was made by order of the great Sultan Bayezid, son of Murad Han, in the year eight hundred and two (AH 802)."

This inscription shows that the building was completed by Sultan Yıldırım Bayezid in AH 802 / AD 1399–1400.

Notably, Bursa Ulucami is counted among the places of worship important for their spiritual eminence. The Masjid al-Haram in Mecca is described as the first station, the Prophet's Mosque in Medina the second, the Al-Aqsa Mosque in Jerusalem the third, the Umayyad Mosque in Damascus the fourth, and Bursa Ulucami the fifth.

In some accounts the fifth station is also given, besides Bursa, to Diyarbakır Ulucami, built by Melikşah, son of the Seljuk Sultan Alparslan, on the plan of the Umayyad Mosque and accepted as the first mosque in Anatolia. Among the people, this standing attributed to Bursa Ulucami rests on an Arabic couplet in the divan of Üftâde Mehmed Muhyiddin, which now hangs in the mosque:

"Yâ câmi'al-kebîr ve yâ mecma'al-kibâr / Tûbâ limen yezûrüke fi'l-leyli ve'n-nehâr.

In meaning: O great and exalted sanctuary, O mosque where the great gather. Glad tidings to those who visit you by night and by day..."

## Mimari | Architecture

Bursa Ulucami is a nearly square rectangular building of about 55 × 69 m. Notable for its twenty domes and prayer area of 3,500 m², the mosque looks almost like a museum of calligraphy, thanks to the inscriptions on its walls and piers, each a work of art.

The mosque has a crown portal on the north façade and entrances on the east and west, three in all. The eastern door is known as the "Bazaar Gate", the western as the "Mufti's" or "Court Gate" and the northern as the "Booksellers' Gate". An additional secondary door was once opened at the southeast corner, where the sultan's lodge stood for a time, but it was closed on the grounds that it was a later addition. That the façade continues in the same system up to the corner with the lodge shows that no original door or window was designed for a sultan's lodge in Bursa Ulucami.

Drawings and photographs by various Turkish and foreign architects in the 1940s and 1950s show that on the east façade a window had been altered under Sultan Abdülaziz into a door for the sultan's lodge. In the 1960 restoration after the bazaar fire, however, the door was removed as "a later addition" and turned back into a window. The original leaves of the cancelled door are kept in the store of the Bursa Regional Directorate of Foundations.

The façades, enlivened by large mouldings, have twenty-six windows in the lower row and thirty-two in the upper. The dome above the twelve-sided marble pool was also left open to ventilate the mosque and bring in daylight. The land of Ulucami belonged to the Orhan Foundation; Yıldırım Bayezid assigned other land to the foundation, and Ulucami was built on this foundation's land.

The domes covering the building, 11 m across, rest on twelve square piers. Each face of the piers carries examples of the Beautiful Names of God in monumental thuluth and kufic. The inscriptions on the piers and walls, which make the interior almost a museum of calligraphy, are by famous calligraphers such as Abdülfettah Efendi, Kazasker Mustafa İzzet Efendi, Sâmi Efendi and Mehmed Şefik Bey. Their outlining and contours were done by Mücellid Mehmed Efendi. The mosque also has a panel by Mahmud II. Placed directly opposite the sultan's lodge, it bears verse 58 of the Surah Nisâ:

"Ve izâ hakemtüm beyne'n-nâsi en tahkümû bi'l-adli. God commands that when you judge between people, you judge with justice."

On the mosque's qibla wall, to the right of the mihrab, the letter "vav" stands out for its grandeur. The beliefs that Hızır prayed in this part and that praying before the letter brings merit are only popular legend.

## Minber | Minbar

The mosque's minbar, made in the kündekâri technique, is a masterpiece consisting entirely of interlocking pieces, with no nails, glue or other binder. The master's inscription on one of the rails of the west-facing balustrades reads, in broken thuluth script, "Amel-i el-Hâc Muhammed bin Abdülazîz ibnü'd-Dakkî", giving the master's name. The same kündekâri master also made the minbar of the Manisa Great Mosque of 1376. The inscription on the Manisa minbar, however, reads "Amel-i el- Hâc Muhammed bin Abdülazîz ibnü'd-Dakkî ve'l-Antebî", adding that the master was from Antep.

The inscription above the door of this minbar, estimated to have taken about three years to complete, reads "Mimmâ amile bi-resmi's-Sultân Bâyezîd bin Murad Hân bi-târîhi seneti isneyni ve semâne mi'e"

In meaning: "Made in the name of Sultan Bayezid bin Murad Han in the year 802", from which the date of construction is understood to be AH 802 / AD 1399–1400.

One of the rarest examples of Ottoman woodwork, this work has come through many ordeals over six hundred and ten years, surviving fires and earthquakes, but it has not escaped damage from unskilled hands.

In particular, wrong interventions in the 19th and 20th centuries damaged parts of the minbar. Even so, it has come down to us as one of the most original examples of the kündekâri art and remains an admired work.

On the east face of the minbar, below its openwork wooden cap, this hadith is written in Seljuk thuluth:

"El-Mü'minü fî'l-mescidi ke's-semeki fi'l-mâi In meaning: "A believer in the mosque is like a fish in water."

On the front of the minbar's pavilion is this hadith: "Men kale lâ ilâhe illâllah Muhammedün Rasûlulllah dehale'l-cenneti. Whoever says there is no god but God and Muhammad is the Messenger of God will enter paradise."

Immediately below, within the pavilion's frame, comes the continuation of the hadith on the east:

"Ve'l-münâfiku fi'l-mescidi ke't-tayri'l-kafesi The hypocrite in the mosque is like a bird in a cage."

The upper part of the minbar's pavilion also bears these words in kufic:

"El-Mülkü Lillâh Sovereignty belongs to God."

One of the minbar's most interesting features is that it consists of 6,666 pieces. Still more striking, the solar system of the sun and planets is depicted on its east face and the galaxy on its west.

## Mihrap, mahfiller ve hat yazıları | Mihrab, galleries and calligraphy

The decoration of the mihrab was commissioned by Tevfik Paşa, exiled to Bursa in 1862. The mihrab is surrounded by four friezes: a band of kufic script, a band of stylised motifs and two of floral decoration. At the two corners of the niche are small columns painted to look like marble. Beside them, as corner fillings, are spandrel designs of large rumi and palmette motifs.

Between these two spandrels stands out a large depiction of a lamp decorated with rumi motifs. In the drop-shaped area at the middle of the lamp is a Qur'anic verse in mirrored thuluth. This decoration is among the most important elements enriching the aesthetic and spiritual atmosphere of Bursa Ulucami.

The plain, elegant muezzins' gallery on eight wooden posts was built by Mustafa Efendi, trustee of the Yıldırım Bayezid foundations. The pediment under its stair carries a quatrain by the poet Rahîmî in talik script. Dated AH 956 / AD 1549, it is the oldest dated inscription in the mosque. During the repairs of 2009, this quatrain and the naturalistic floral decoration, painted on leather, on the side of the stair to the gallery were fully revealed.

The rococo marble preacher's chair opposite the muezzins' gallery bears a couplet in talik script, dated AH 1231 / AD 1816. There is also a wooden preacher's chair beside the mihrab.

The sultan's lodge at the mosque's southeast corner was rebuilt by Sultan Abdülaziz in 1862, of wood and in a very plain style. The section beneath it had long been used as a library. Münzevî Abdullah Efendi founded a library here in 1786, donated his own books, and also bought and endowed the books of Yenişehirli Hacı Osman Efendi.

With about one hundred and seventy calligraphic works, painted on the walls or hung as panels, Ulucami holds a distinctive place in Islamic mosque architecture. It is not known for certain whether these inscriptions date from the mosque's first construction. Known to have existed in the 16th century, these works were last repaired after the 1855 earthquake; some were restored, and others erased and rewritten.

The façades, enlivened by large mouldings, have fifty-eight windows in all: twenty-six in the lower row and thirty-two in the upper. The top of the fountain dome was also left open to ventilate the mosque and bring in daylight.

## Havuzlu şadırvan | Pool fountain

One of the striking elements of Ulucami's layout is the pool fountain at its centre. Stories are told that the site was "the property of a non-Muslim woman", but since there can be no private ownership on foundation land, the claim has no historical basis. The land on which Ulucami was built belonged to the Orhan Gazi Foundation. Yıldırım Bayezid assigned other land to the Orhan Foundation, and as a result the mosque was built on this land. The story is thus seen rather as one of the legends produced by the city's memory.

The pool is sixteen-sided and faced with Marmara marble slabs. Each side has a Baroque motif with a tap in the middle. At its centre, Pınarbaşı water flows from a three-tiered fountain; this arrangement is both an aesthetic element and an emphasis on the cooling, purifying function of the space. Leaving open the centre of the dome above the pool is a deliberate design choice that lights and ventilates the interior. The sound of flowing water adds a symbolic depth that deepens the spiritual atmosphere in the mosque.

Evliya Çelebi gives a detailed description of Ulucami from his journey to Bursa in AH 1040 / AD 1630. According to him, this mosque built by Yıldırım Bayezid stands on a high, airy spot in the city and recalls Kahkaha Castle in its majesty. Inside rise square piers whose lower parts are striking with painted and gilded decoration. On them "Yâ Hannân, Yâ Mennân, Yâ Deyyân, Yâ Sübhân" is written in different calligraphic styles. The alifs of the letters are three zirâ (about 55 cm each) long, and the style reflects that of the calligrapher Müsta'sımi.

Evliya Çelebi reports nineteen domes in the mosque, covered with lead. He likens their finials to a shining sun. The central twentieth dome was left open to light the mosque, and its top was covered with a mesh of yellow brass wire to keep out birds and wild animals. Beneath this dome is a pool ten cubits in width and length, considered suitable for ablutions under the Hanafi school.

Evliya Çelebi also specifically notes that many kinds of fish swam in the pool and that the congregation performed their worship after taking ablutions from this water.

## Minareler | Minarets

Of Ulucami's two minarets, the western one is known to have been built by Yıldırım Bayezid. The eastern minaret was planned at the first construction but not completed. Because it was used half-finished for some time, it was called the "Güdük" (Stumpy) Minaret. It is thought to have been completed later, under Çelebi Sultan Mehmed or Yavuz Sultan Selim.

The bases and plinths of the minarets are marble and their round shafts brick. Both balconies rest on six rows of stalactites. The caps were originally wood covered with lead and were rebuilt in stone after the last fire.

## Onarımlar | Repairs

A building of this size inevitably needed repair many times. Various parts of the mosque were repaired on 13 Cemaziyelevvel 899 / 19 February 1494, 28 Cemaziyelahir 959 / 18 December 1503, 5 Zilhicce 970 / 26 July 1563 and 13 Cemaziyelevvel 975 / 15 December 1567. In addition, on 29 Cemaziyelevvel 1081 / 14 October 1670, lightning struck the minaret, and its wooden cap, lead covering and copper finial were renewed.

On 22 Cemaziyelahir 1145 / 10 December 1732 the minaret cap, two fountains, water channels, the glass and wire of one hundred and twenty-nine dome windows across twenty domes, and the kufic, monumental and other inscriptions on the piers were repaired. The two large fountains in the courtyard, their domes, the roof and painted ceilings of the two large platforms beside the mosque and the ceiling to its west were also repaired. This document shows that the mosque had painted decoration as well as calligraphy, and that its courtyard had platforms with wooden eaves. It was repaired once more on 20 Rebiyülevvel 1150 / 18 July 1737.

Ulucami had the greatest repair of its six-hundred-year history after the 1855 earthquake; revived after years of very costly work, it reopened for worship in 1862. In the 1958 Bursa Bazaar fire its north façade was damaged and so faced with stone. On the other three façades the plaster was scraped off, giving the building its present appearance.

The mosque's grandeur continues to be preserved through its most recent restoration, in 2009.

## Çevresindeki hayratlar | Charitable works around it

As at Emir Sultan, many charitable works such as fountains were built around Ulucami. The four-sided fountain at the building's southwest corner was first built in 1903 but demolished as not original during the mosque's major repair of 1959. It was later rebuilt in 1993–1994.

The fountain's inscription in monumental thuluth carries this verse from the Surah Enbiyâ:

"Veca'alnâ mine'l-mâi külle şey'in hayy We made every living thing from water."

The inscription continues: "Built by Ahmed Münir Paşa, Governor of Hüdavendigâr, and rebuilt by Bursa Municipality. First built AH 1321, second AD 1994." Relief decoration has been added on both sides of the inscription's first line.

The pediments on the north and south faces bear the words "Muhammedun Resûlullah sallallâhu aleyhi ve sellem" in the form of a tughra. The tughras are set in a crown decorated with leaf motifs, with tile plates on either side. The panel stone is entirely of marble.

The troughs on the fountain's other three faces rest on two marble feet. The panel stone is faced with patterned Kütahya tiles, and the corners are formed by pink and white marble set one above the other.

Each marble column ends in a pink marble capital and has two round-cornered wooden brackets, as if carrying the roof. The ceiling of the roof, resting on wooden feet, is decorated with square laths and covered above with copper.

According to the Bursa registers, with donations from Şeyhülislam Karaçelebizâde Abdülaziz Efendi, fountains were built in the courtyard as well as the one still standing at the mosque's southwest corner.

Three fountains stand in a line in the mosque's northern courtyard. The two at the east and west of the courtyard were reworked in 2011. They were built in keeping with their style in old photographs, with an onion dome on eight wooden posts joined by trefoil arches.

The fountain in the middle, entirely of marble and 2.70 × 1.15 m, free-standing with four faces, was commissioned by Governor Ahmed Münir Paşa.

## Vakıf ve akar | Waqf and akar

To provide Ulucami with an income, Yıldırım Bayezid also built the Bedesten nearby; the Bedesten is the core of today's Bursa Covered Bazaar.
