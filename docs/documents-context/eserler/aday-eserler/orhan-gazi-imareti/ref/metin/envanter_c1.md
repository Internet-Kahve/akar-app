ORHAN BEY CAMİİ
Orhan Bey Camii, Osmangazi ilçesi, Nalbantoğlu Mahallesi, Belediye
Caddesi, No: 7’ de yer almaktadır.
Tarihi Çarşılar ve Hanlar Bölgesi içinde bulunan cami, Ulucami’nin doğusunda, tarihi belediye binasının ise batısında konumlanmaktadır. Osmanlı
Devleti’nin kurucusu Osman Bey’in oğlu Orhan Bey tarafından yaptırılan
külliye bünyesinde, H. 740 / M. 1339-40 yılında inşa edilmiştir.
Osmanlı Devleti’nin ilk külliyelerinden biri olan bu yapı topluluğundan günümüze yalnızca Orhan Camii, Emir Hanı ve Orhan Hamamı ulaşabilmiştir.
İmaret ve mektep yapıları ise zamanla yok olmuştur.
Gazi Orhan Bey, ağabeyi Alaaddin Bey (Paşa)’in beylikten feragat etmesi
üzerine 1326 yılında devletin başına geçmiştir. Aynı yıl fethettiği Bursa’yı
başkent ilan ederek yönetim merkezini buraya taşımış, sur dışında inşa ettirdiği bu külliye ile şehri imar ederken aynı zamanda büyüterek bir çekim
merkezi haline getirecek çalışmalar yürütmüştür.

ORHAN BEY MOSQUE
Orhan Bey Mosque is located in Osmangazi District, Nalbantoğlu Neighborhood, on Belediye Street, No. 7.
The mosque, located in the Historical Bazaars and Hans District, is located
to the east of the Ulucami and to the west of the historical town hall. It was
built in AH 740 / AD 1339-40, within the complex built by Orhan Bey,
the son of Osman Bey, the founder of the Ottoman Empire.
Of this group of buildings, one of the first complexes of the Ottoman Empire, only the Orhan Mosque, Emir Han, and Orhan Bath have survived to
the present day. The imaret and school buildings have disappeared over time.
Gazi Orhan Bey became the head of the state in 1326 after his elder brother Alâaddin Bey (Paşa) renounced the beylik (principaility). He declared
Bursa, which he conquered in the same year, as the capital and moved the
administrative center there. He built a complex outside the city walls, while
also carrying out works to expand the city and make it a center of attraction.

He increased the principality’s lands, which he inherited from his father as
6,000 km², sixteen-fold, reaching 95,000 km². Orhan Gazi, who died in
1360 at the age of 79, was buried in his tomb within the Hisar in the Saint
Elias Monastery, which he converted into a mosque and madrasa after
the conquest.

During the 31-day siege of Bursa by Karamanoğlu Mehmed Bey in 1413,
this social complex, like many other structures outside the city walls, was
damaged by burning, but was later repaired by Sultan Çelebi Mehmed and

It reads:
“The construction of this honorable structure was ordered by the sultan of the
veterans and mujahids, Orhan Bey, son of Osman Bey -may Allah exalt the souls
of both- in the year (AH 740 / AD 1339-40). Then Karamanoğlu launched an

Osmanlı’nın ilk dönem tabhaneli/zaviyeli camileri arasında yer alan bu yapı,
simetrik plan şemasıyla dikkat çeker. Yan mekânlar aynalı tonozlarla, diğer
bölümler ise kubbelerle örtülmüştür. Beş gözlü son cemaat mahalli, 25.80
× 6.30 m ölçülerindedir ve tamamen kubbelerle örtülüdür. Ana mekân,
kare planlı ardışık iki bölümden oluşmakta, bu bölümlerin doğu ve batısında
dikdörtgen planlı tabhaneler yer almaktadır. Tabhanelere kuzeyden bitişik,
dikdörtgen planlı ve tonoz örtülü iki oda eşlik eder. Bu kurguyla cami hem
ibadet hem de tekke işlevlerini aynı bünyede barındıran erken Osmanlı
mimarisinin karakteristik örneklerinden biridir.

This symmetrically planned structure, an example of the early Ottoman
zawiya/hospice mosques, has side sections covered with flattened barrel
vaults vaults and the other sections covered with domes. The narthex,
consisting of two consecutive square-plan spaces covered with domes and
measuring 25.80 × 6.30 m with five bays, is completed by rectangular-plan
hospices to the east and west of the main dome and two rectangular-plan
vaulted rooms adjacent to them from the north. With this design, the
mosque, which incorporates the functions of both a place of worship and
a Sufi lodge in the same structure, is one of the characteristic examples of
early Ottoman architecture.
