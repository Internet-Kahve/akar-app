# Eyne Bey (İnebey) Madrasa · About (English)

Translation of `../eser-hakkinda.md`, paragraph by paragraph. Sources and trust labels come from the Turkish record.

## Tarihçe | History

The madrasa is at İnebey Sokak No. 5, İnebey Caddesi, Tahtakale neighbourhood, Osmangazi district.

In the literature it is also known as the "Stepped Madrasa", the "Great İnebey Madrasa" and the "Hüseyin Çelebi Madrasa". No inscription from the building has survived.

It was built in the early 15th century by Eyne Bey b. Feleküddin, governor of the Karesi province under Sultan Murad I Hüdavendigâr and subaşı (city governor) under Yıldırım Bayezid and his sons.

Eyne Bey fought in many battles, notably Kosovo, and during the Interregnum after Yıldırım Bayezid's reign he sided with Mehmed Çelebi. He died of a sword wound received in the war that ended in İsa Çelebi's defeat, and his grave is in the village of Tekke, Kepsut district, Balıkesir.

## Vakfiye | Endowment deed

According to the endowment deed of 3 Şaban 804 / 8 March 1402, certified by the Bursa judge Mehmed bin Hamza bin Mehmed (Molla Fenârî), baths, mills, vineyards, gardens, lands and villages in Bursa, Balıkesir, Bergama, Susurluk and Ankara were assigned in the name of Subaşı Eyne Bey bin Feleküddin, with the madrasa he built in Bursa and the zawiya in Balıkesir to be supported by the foundation's income.

The same deed states that the teacher at the Bursa madrasa was to be paid 10 dirhams a day from the income of the bath, also in Bursa. Twelve students staying in the madrasa's twelve rooms were each allotted one and a half dirhams a day. The deed also provides that five hafizes from among Eyne Bey's slaves would recite the Qur'an every day in the foundation's tomb in the madrasa garden, for two dirhams each, 10 dirhams in all.

## Hüseyin Çelebi onarımı | The Hüseyin Çelebi repair

In AH 1085 / AD 1674 the madrasa was thoroughly repaired by Hüseyin Çelebi Paşa, a relative of the grand vizier Köprülü Fazıl Ahmed Paşa and the mystic Niyâzî-i Mısrî. During this work the madrasa was given a library, very valuable for its time. This library later moved to Orhan Mosque, and from then on the literature calls the building the "Amcazâde Hüseyin Çelebi Madrasa".

## Mimari | Architecture

The building's rectangular plan runs north to south, and its main walls are of alternating rubble stone and brick. The depressed-arched main entrance on the north façade is reached by stairs of seven steps on either side. This façade has three windows in all, including one on each side of the door. The windows are rectangular with stone frames, pointed-arched brick pediments and iron bars.

The courtyard at the centre of the madrasa is rectangular, 8.00 × 10.00 m. At its centre is an octagonal fountain with a jet. Behind the arcades around the courtyard are nine student rooms, a main classroom and, to the north, a later library.

The courtyard is surrounded by arcades of pointed arches carried by twelve columns of küfeki stone. These arcades form a route of movement around the courtyard.

The student rooms measure on average 3.10 × 3.45 m, with similar varying sizes. Each cell has a cupboard, a hearth, two shelf niches and a lamp niche. The classroom is square, 6.20 × 6.20 m inside, and covered by a lead-clad dome on an octagonal drum. Saw-tooth eaves are used at the transitions between square and dome and along the roof edges. The classroom interior has painted decoration on various surfaces.

The roofs above the student cells and arcades are also lead-covered and finished with saw-tooth eaves. The south façade has four windows in all, three in the lower row and one in the middle of the main classroom's front. They are rectangular with stone frames and pointed-arched brick pediments, all fitted with iron bars.

The smaller rectangular window above has a pointed-arched pediment and a plaster grille. To the west of the same façade, lower and larger than the windows, is the place of a pointed-arched door later filled in with brick. It is thought to be the door to the madrasa's small garden mentioned in the sources.

At the madrasa's northwest corner is the library of 7.00 × 6.00 m, added in AH 1085 / AD 1674. It is reached by a passage added to the wall to the right of the madrasa entrance, climbed by stairs set inside the thinned wall. The library's east façade has two windows, a large one below and a small one above: rectangular, with stone frames, pointed-arched brick pediments and iron bars. The same façade also has the place of a fountain in a round-arched niche, though its original elements have not survived.

## Kütüphaneye dönüşüm ve bugün | Becoming a library, and today

After madrasas were closed in 1924, the Evkaf Administration sold the building, which served as a canning factory until 1962. The Foundations Administration later acknowledged that this sale had been a mistake and bought the building back.

In 1969, when the manuscripts and printed works of the library in Orhan Mosque moved to the madrasa, it reopened as the "Bursa Manuscript and Old Printed Works Library".

Since 2010 it has been organised as the "Bursa İnebey Manuscript Library Directorate", under the Istanbul Regional Directorate of the Turkish Manuscripts Institution.

The madrasa has undergone both extensive and partial repairs over time. The most extensive were in 1683 and the 1960s. Between 2007 and 2010 it went through another detailed restoration.

Today it continues to serve with its many valuable manuscripts and printed works.
